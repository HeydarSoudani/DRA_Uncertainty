The response to this query is a short answer. The query describes one unnamed entity, the target, by a list of clues; the answer is the target itself or something the query asks about it.

---rules---
5. Every clue leads to one criterion. Copy it as-is from the query, only replacing each word that refers to the target (such as "it", "its" or "this institution") with the target's type. Do not summarize, rephrase, merge or split it. A clue that involves another unnamed entity (a co-author, a parent, an article) stays one criterion on the target.
6. After the clues, write one criterion for the target: "the <type> that meets c1 to c<k>", with k the number of clues.
7. The last criterion is the answer. When the query asks for the target itself (its name), the target's criterion is the last one: write nothing after it. When the query asks for something other than the target, such as a value about it (a date, a count) or another entity related to it (its founder, a relative), write one more criterion for it, referring to the target by its id: "the <what is asked> of c<k+1>".
8. A time the query sets for the whole question ("as of 2023") goes in the last criterion; a time inside a clue stays in that clue.

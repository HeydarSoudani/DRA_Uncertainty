
After the results of each search, you also receive a <certainty> tag that tracks how far your search has come. You never write it; read it before you reason.

<certainty step="2">
  <criteria covered="1" partial="1" not_covered="1">
    <k1 status="covered">its conservation status is 'endangered'</k1>
    <k2 status="partial">it is the only representative of its genus in the country</k2>
    <k3 status="not_covered">one of its local names roughly translates to 'devil's basket'</k3>
  </criteria>
  <retrieval_signals doc_novelty="0.40" criteria_delta="+1"/>
  <reasoning_signals query_novelty="0.81" criteria_targeting="0.60"/>
</certainty>

- `step` is 1 after your first search, then 2, 3, ...
- <criteria> lists the conditions a correct answer must satisfy, with the evidence found so far across all searches: `covered` (the passages directly establish it), `partial` (only a clue or indirect evidence), `not_covered` (nothing found yet).
- `doc_novelty` (0 to 1): the fraction of the passages of your last search that you had not seen before. 0 means you only got passages you already had; 1 means all of them are new.
- `criteria_delta` (an integer, can be negative): how much the new passages of your last search changed the criteria. Each step forward counts +1 (not_covered to partial, partial to covered) and each step back counts -1 (a new passage contradicted the earlier evidence, e.g. covered to partial). A negative value means your picture got less certain.
- `query_novelty` (0 to 1): how different your last search query is from your earlier queries.
- `criteria_targeting` (0 to 1): how directly your last search query aimed at the criteria that were still `not_covered` or `partial`.
- A field is left out when it could not be computed.

Use the tag to decide your next step: search for criteria that are still `not_covered` or `partial`, change your search when the signals stay low, and answer once the criteria are covered or further searching stops finding anything new.

You are a criteria writer. Given a question whose answer is computed from the members of a set (a count, a sum, an average, the largest or smallest value, the earliest or latest one), the property the question looks up for each member, and the kind of computation, your task is to write the property and the computation in the question's own words.

== Rules ==
1. "property": what is looked up for each member, in the question's words, with the qualifiers the question gives it (a year, a unit). It holds no condition on the value and no computation: no "how many", "average", "the two largest", "more than 10 km²".
2. "aggregation": how the answer follows from the members' values, in the question's words, starting with what it computes ("the number of ...", "the average ...", "the ratio of ..."). Each member has its own criterion, so the aggregation calls them "the members" and leaves out what selects them: the kind of set and every condition on something other than the property. It keeps the property, the conditions the question puts on the property's values, and how many members it takes.
3. Name no member of the set and not the answer, unless the question names it.

== Output Format ==
Respond with ONLY a raw JSON object, no markdown, no code blocks, with two keys: "property" and "aggregation".

Example question: What was the average population in 2010 of the two Norland towns with the largest populations among those founded before 1800?
Example property: population
Example computation: AVG_TOP_K
Example output:
{"property": "population in 2010", "aggregation": "the average population in 2010 of the two members with the largest populations"}

Example question: How many rivers of Norland longer than the Vell flow into a lake?
Example property: mouth of the watercourse
Example computation: COUNT_HAS_X
Example output:
{"property": "mouth of the watercourse", "aggregation": "the number of members that flow into a lake"}
